Internal DSRC fault — tachograph error code 84

Truck Data TechnologyUpdated September 20263 min read

Code 84 means your tachograph has found an internal fault in its DSRC module — the unit used for remote roadside checks — or a problem with the DSRC antenna.

Severity
Low

Does not affect the recording of your driving time, breaks or rest.

Affected module
DSRC

The module used for remote communication with control authorities at roadside checks.

Keep driving?
Yes

Acknowledge the message and book a check with an authorised workshop.

What it means

What this code means

DSRC stands for Dedicated Short-Range Communication. It is the module in your digital tachograph that lets roadside enforcement officers interrogate the unit remotely — without stopping your vehicle — as you pass a control point.

Code 84, internal DSRC fault, means the tachograph has detected an internal fault on this module, or that the DSRC antenna is faulty or not connected. According to the manual, the severity of this fault is Low.

What to do now

What to do now

The manual gives one instruction for code 84:

  1. Acknowledge the message on the tachograph display.
  2. Have the DSRC module, cabling and antenna checked at an authorised workshop.

There is no requirement to stop driving. Book the workshop visit at your next opportunity.

Why it happens

Why it happens

According to the manual, code 84 has two possible causes:

  • an internal fault within the DSRC module itself, or
  • a fault on the DSRC antenna, or the antenna is not connected.

Because the DSRC module only handles remote roadside interrogation, a fault here does not interrupt the normal recording of driving, other work, availability or rest.

Severity

Can you keep driving?

Yes. Code 84 is classified as Low severity in the manual. It does not affect the tachograph's core recording function, and there is no requirement to stop the vehicle.

The only practical effect is that remote DSRC interrogation at a roadside control point may not work correctly until the module is repaired — in that case an inspector simply carries out a normal in-cab check instead. Acknowledge the message and have the DSRC module, cabling and antenna checked at your next authorised workshop visit.

Questions & answers

Frequently asked questions

What does DSRC mean on a tachograph?

DSRC stands for Dedicated Short-Range Communication. It is the module that lets roadside enforcement officers interrogate your tachograph remotely, without stopping your vehicle, as you pass a control point.

Can I keep driving with an internal DSRC fault (code 84)?

Yes. The manual classifies code 84 as Low severity. It does not affect the recording of driving time, breaks or rest — acknowledge the message and book a workshop check.

Does code 84 affect my driver card data?

No. The DSRC module only handles remote roadside interrogation — it plays no part in recording your driving, other work, availability or rest, which is why the manual rates this fault Low severity.

What should I do if code 84 keeps coming back?

Have the DSRC module, cabling and antenna checked at an authorised workshop, as instructed by the manual. A recurring fault points to a wiring or antenna connection problem rather than a one-off glitch.
